Click to fold it back into the corner.

Your screen has four corners. Put one to work.

CornerWindow is a tiny black quarter-circle in a corner of your Linux desktop. Click it and it springs open into brightness, sound, media, toggles and a command bar. Click anywhere else and it folds away.

Install Source on GitHub

Runs on GNOME, KDE, Hyprland, Sway and more, on X11 or Wayland. Free software under GPL-3.0.

Closed, it still does things.

The trigger stays almost invisible. It grows a little under the pointer and shows a faint rim of light. You don't have to open it for the everyday stuff.

Gestures on the closed trigger
ScrollMonitor brightness
Shift + scrollVolume
Middle-clickMute output
Right-clickPlay or pause media

When something changes, the trigger grows to show an arc and the value. A ring means a timer is running, a red dot means your mic is muted, a yellow dot means Awake is on. It hides while a fullscreen game or video has focus.

Open, it's a panel of cards.

Each card checks whether it works on your machine and hides itself if it doesn't. Arrange them in any columns you like.

Display
Brightness and contrast over DDC/CI, so the monitor itself changes, not a dimming filter. Presets for day, evening, night and movies.
Sound
Volume, mic mute and a switcher for the output device.
Now playing
Artwork, title, controls and progress for any MPRIS player. Only there while something plays.
Quick toggles
Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, night light, dark mode, focus, awake, mic mute and Liquid Glass.
System
CPU, GPU, memory and network sparklines, disk space and uptime.
Drawer
A timer, your clipboard history and a scratchpad that saves itself.
Power
Lock, sleep, restart and shut down. Each one needs a press-and-hold, so nothing happens by accident.
Weather
Current conditions from Open-Meteo. Off until you give it a location.

Or just start typing.

With the panel open, type anywhere and the command bar picks it up. Numbers set things, words apply presets, and sums get answered. Enter runs it, or copies the answer.

Command bar examples
b 40Set brightness to 40
nightApply the Night preset
timer 10Start a 10-minute timer
muteMute output
2*(3+4)Calculate; Enter copies the answer

c, v, t, lock and more are in there too.

Make it glass.

The island is solid black by default. Switch on Liquid Glass and the panel becomes a lens over whatever is behind it. It is not a blur with a white wash.

brightnessvolumenight lighttimerclipboardnow playing

Drag the glass.

  • Lensing. Near the rim the backdrop bends inward like a thick convex edge, with a slight colour fringe where it bends most. The middle stays undistorted.
  • Frost and tint. Anywhere from clear to frosted, and from see-through to easy to read.
  • Specular rim. A thin white edge and a soft inner glow, lit from an angle you choose.
  • Light and dark. Follows your desktop's setting, or picks by how bright the content behind it is.

Turn it on with the Glass quick toggle, in Settings, or with cornerwindow set panel.glass true. It switches instantly.

On X11 the backdrop is live: a video or a window you drag shows through as it moves. When nothing behind changes, nothing is redrawn.

Everything moves on springs.

Each animation is a spring with a response and a damping, ticked at your monitor's refresh rate. Click the trigger again halfway through and it turns around with its speed intact instead of snapping.

  • The window is sized once and never resized or moved while it animates. Everything outside the outline is transparent and click-through.
  • Cards are drawn once into an image during the morph. Nothing is laid out again mid-animation.
  • Nothing ticks while the panel is closed, so it uses 0% CPU at idle.

Click the little screen to open and close it with these settings. Try damping below 0.5.

Install it.

You need Python 3.11 or newer. The script fetches PyGObject and pycairo from your distro (apt, dnf, pacman or zypper) and does the rest.

git clone https://github.com/MightyTuniek/cornerwindow
cd cornerwindow
./install.sh
  • Asks for sudo once to add a udev rule, so you can talk to your monitor over I²C without root. For an external monitor, DDC/CI must be on in its own menu. Most ship with it on.
  • Builds the Liquid Glass pixel kernel if a C compiler is present.
  • Puts cornerwindow in ~/.local/bin and starts it with your session. On GNOME, Super + \ opens and closes it.
  • Finishes with cornerwindow doctor, which tells you what works and how to fix what doesn't.

Flags: --no-sudo, --no-hotkey, --uninstall.

From the command line

# what works, and how to fix what doesn't
cornerwindow doctor
cornerwindow toggle
cornerwindow set brightness 40
cornerwindow set brightness +10
cornerwindow get contrast
cornerwindow action display.preset.night
cornerwindow settings display

In a config file

Defaults, then a file for this PC, then yours. Later layers win. The settings window writes only what you changed and keeps your comments.

# ~/.config/cornerwindow/config.toml
[general]
corner = "top-left"

[trigger]
radius = 16
scroll = "audio.volume.step"

[modules]
columns = [["header", "display", "audio"],
           ["toggles", "drawer", "power"]]
